Tim Exile

IDM / drum & bass / live electronics · England

Tim Exile is English producer Timothy Charles Shaw, who started on classical violin at 12 and spent the 2000s moving from dark drum & bass on Moving Shadow through warped IDM on Planet Mu to a genuinely pop-leaning record on Warp. The real draw was always the live show — entirely improvised, built from looped and pitch-shifted vocals, gabba beats and whatever synth textures he's cooking up in the moment.

Did you knowHe got so fed up with ordinary DJing that he built his own performance software from scratch, and Native Instruments turned it into the commercial plugins The Finger and The Mouth. He later rebuilt the idea as Endlesss, a remote jamming app that blew up during lockdown with artists like Imogen Heap jumping in.
Start here
Listening Tree2009
His Warp debut and most celebrated record — the one where the noise finally turns into pop songs.

The Streets

UK garage / British rap · Birmingham, England

The Streets is Mike Skinner, a Birmingham lad who moved to Brixton at the turn of the millennium with a laptop and basically invented a whole new way of rapping. He spits over 2-step UK garage beats in his own thick geezer accent, about pints, kebabs, PlayStation and skint Fridays. Not grime, not garage, not hip-hop, but somehow all three at once — and nobody's sounded quite like it since.

Did you knowHe recorded his debut in a Brixton flat using an emptied-out wardrobe stuffed with duvets as his vocal booth, and the whole thing cost under £4,000 to make. He still writes his lyrics as text messages to himself.
Start here
Original Pirate Material2002
The album that turned everyday British life into pop art — still sounds like nothing else over 20 years on.

Praful

Chill-out nu-jazz / sax & electronica · Amsterdam, Netherlands

Praful is a German-born saxophonist who finished a jazz degree in Amsterdam and then took a very unusual detour before his career really began. He blends jazz, acid jazz, Latin rhythms and electronica into something warm, breathy and unhurried — smoky sax and bansuri flute floating over soft electronic grooves. Built for a slow Sunday morning.

Did you knowStraight out of jazz school he spent six months in an Osho ashram in India studying the bansuri flute, and came back with a new name. Years later his single 'Sigh' camped out on the Billboard charts for over 70 weeks and landed him a US deal on a label co-run by Dave Koz.

One Day Deep2001
Eleven tracks of smoky, chilled-out sax and electronics — the record that made American radio fall for his Dutch-Indian-German hybrid sound.

Glitch-hop / jungle with angelic vocals · Chicago, USA

Your Infamous Harp is the husband-and-wife duo of Andy and Sara Domin, who've been making music together since 2006 and somehow turn skittering IDM, jungle breakbeats and glitch-hop into something genuinely beautiful. Fatboy Slim

Big beat / electronic dance · Brighton, England

Fatboy Slim is Norman Cook, the cheeky Brighton legend who basically invented the big-beat sound and turned the late-'90s into one giant sweaty dancefloor. He chops up funk, hip-hop, soul and acid house into gloriously chaotic, hook-stuffed bangers that feel like a party crammed into three minutes. Orbital

Rave-era techno / electronica · Sevenoaks, Kent, England

Orbital are brothers Phil and Paul Hartnoll, who came out of the M25 rave scene in 1989 and became the act that made techno work as an album, a headline set and a proper emotional experience. Their sound is big, melodic and euphoric — acid squelch, cinematic strings and breakbeats building into the goosebump moments that made their Glastonbury 1994 set a legend. Thirty-odd years on they still headline festivals in the same torch glasses.

Did you knowThe name comes from the M25, the orbital motorway that ferried ravers to illegal parties around London. Their breakthrough single 'Chime' was recorded on their dad's cassette deck — and at the 2012 Paralympics opening ceremony Stephen Hawking joined them on stage wearing their trademark torch glasses.
Start here
Orbital 21993
The Brown Album — 'Halcyon + On + On', 'Lush 3' and 'Impact' in one place; the record that proved dance music could be an album.

Ruby My Dear

Melodic breakcore / IDM / jungle · Toulouse, France

Ruby My Dear is Julien Chastagnol, a Toulouse producer who took breakcore — the fastest, most chaotic corner of electronic music — and taught it to sing. Under the frantic drum programming sit haunting piano lines, dream-like atmospheres and real instruments, so every track feels like a film noir that suddenly breaks into a sprint. If you thought this stuff was just noise, he's the one who'll change your mind.

Did you knowHis name is a Thelonious Monk ballad and his debut album title nods to Ornette Coleman's The Shape of Jazz to Come — a breakcore producer who thinks like a jazz musician. He's also Igorrr's regular partner in crime: the two made the album Maigre together in 2014.

Lu:k

Lo-fi bedroom jungle / early Estonian D&B · Estonia

Picture teenage kids in mid-'90s Estonia — a dirt-poor, brand-new republic — taping UK jungle off Finnish late-night radio because that was the only way to hear it, then rebuilding it on entry-level software, custom-made soundcards and PCs that bandmate Tõnis Valk put together himself. That's Lu:k — Virko Veskoja and friends, quietly inventing Estonian jungle and drum & bass between 1994 and 1997, before it even had a name here.

Did you knowThe name 'Lu:k' was picked to mean nothing — it happens to be Estonian for 'hatch' or 'trap door'. Their two cassette albums and one 12" vanished for decades until Tallinn label Memme Vaev pressed eight of the tracks to vinyl in 2025.
Start here
Lu:k2025 · tapes from 1994–97
Eight rescued cuts, from the Minnie Riperton-sampling '94 stepper 'Lovin U' to the rave-era breakbeat of 'Kadunud leitud' — pure lo-fi bedroom jungle, weirdly ahead of its time.

Ott

Lush psychedelic dub / psy-dub · Somerset, United Kingdom

Ott (born Ott Langrell) is the undisputed wizard of psychedelic dub, building gorgeous, bass-heavy, kaleidoscopic soundscapes that wrap around you like a warm blanket. His music is impossibly detailed and trippy without ever being cheesy — all melting echoes and organic textures. If you've ever wanted dub reggae to take you to outer space, this is your guy.

Did you knowBefore going solo he engineered and produced for Sinéad O'Connor, The Orb and Brian Eno, and he's a longtime collaborator of Shpongle's Simon Posford. His Hallucinogen 'In Dub' project basically reinvented psytrance as slow-motion dub and became a genre landmark.

Hidden Orchestra

Cinematic jazz-noir / electronica · Edinburgh, Scotland

Hidden Orchestra is the studio brainchild of multi-instrumentalist Joe Acheson, who records musicians one at a time and stitches them together as one imaginary ensemble. The result is lush, brooding cinematic music swirling jazz, classical, dub, drum-and-bass and film-score drama into something spine-tingling. Live, it explodes into a dual-drummer powerhouse.

Did you knowAcheson is obsessed with field recordings — his work 'Wingbeats' was built almost entirely from manipulated bird songs, and he'll happily sample a cooker timer or a creaking board if he likes the sound. The 'orchestra' is hidden because it never actually plays in the same room.
Start here
Archipelago2012
Modal jazz, Sufi rhythms, field recordings — their most celebrated album pulls from everywhere and somehow lands perfectly. Put it on and just let it take you somewhere.

Acoustic soul with jazz roots · Freiburg, Germany

Tok Tok Tok was a German duo built around the velvet-warm voice of Tokunbo Akinro and the multi-instrumental wizardry of Morten Klein (sax, guitar, drums, even mouth percussion). Belleruche

Trip-hop / 'turntable soul' · London, England

Belleruche are a gloriously analog London trio who basically invented their own genre — 'turntable soul music.' Picture Kathrin deBoer's smoky, Sarah-Vaughan-ish vocals draped over Ricky Fabulous's bluesy guitar and DJ Modest's crackly, hands-on scratching. Warm and dusty like Portishead or Morcheeba, but way more playful and live-sounding. Hear one track and you'll want the whole crate.

Did you knowThe band formed by accident — Ricky and Modest were doing turntable-and-guitar sets in London bars, bumped into Kathrin at a market, and she sang over a cup of tea. Their track 'Northern Girls' later soundtracked Mercedes-Benz's 125th-anniversary UK ads.
Start here
Turntable Soul Music2007
Their debut and instant cult classic — the fastest-selling debut in Tru Thoughts' history — capturing everything that makes them special.

Skalpel is the Wrocław duo of Marcin Cichy & Igor Pudło — two crate-digging producers who turned dusty 1960s–70s Polish jazz LPs into smoky, head-nodding hip-hop beats.
